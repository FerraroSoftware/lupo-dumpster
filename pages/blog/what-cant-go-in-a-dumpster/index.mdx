## Why Some Items Are Banned From Dumpsters

It's not about making your life harder. There are real reasons these items need their own disposal.

### Landfill rules

Your dumpster load goes to a landfill or transfer station, and those facilities have rules about what they'll accept. Hazardous materials can contaminate soil and groundwater. That matters a lot in Florida, where sandy soil lets liquids soak down quickly and many communities depend on groundwater.

### Safety

Some of these items are dangerous in a closed load:

- **Propane tanks** can leak or explode if they're crushed or get hot. A dumpster sitting in the Florida sun gets hot.
- **Batteries**, especially lithium-ion batteries from tools and electronics, can spark and start fires when they're damaged.
- **Solvents and chemicals** can react with each other or leak onto other debris.
- **Medical waste**, like needles, can injure the people who handle the load.

### Equipment and processing

Tires don't break down in a landfill, and many facilities don't want them in mixed loads. Wet paint can spill and coat everything around it.

## How to Dispose of Prohibited Items Safely

Each item on the list has a better place to go. Rules and locations vary, so check with your county or city for current drop-off sites and hours.

| Item | Where it usually goes |
| --- | --- |
| Hazardous chemicals and solvents | Your county's household hazardous waste (HHW) facility or collection event |
| Wet paint | County HHW facility. Some areas allow fully dried latex paint in the trash, so check locally. |
| Tires | Tire retailers (many take old tires, sometimes for a fee) or your county's tire drop-off |
| Batteries and propane tanks | County HHW facility, battery recycling drop-offs at some retailers, or propane exchange and refill locations |
| Medical waste | Pharmacy take-back programs, sharps containers, or your county's guidance on sharps disposal |

### Hazardous chemicals and solvents

This includes things like pool chemicals, pesticides, motor oil, gasoline, paint thinner, and strong cleaners. Tampa Bay–area counties generally offer household hazardous waste drop-off for residents, so check with your county for locations and hours. Keep products in their original containers, close the lids tightly, and don't mix anything together.

### Wet paint

Liquid paint, stain, and varnish need to go to a household hazardous waste site. If you have a little latex paint left over, some areas let you dry it out with kitty litter or a paint hardener before throwing out the can. Check with your county or city before you do, and ask us before putting any paint cans in your dumpster.

### Tires

Many tire shops will take old tires when you buy new ones, and some accept them separately. Your county may also have a tire drop-off. Never leave tires on the curb or in the woods. They collect rainwater and become mosquito breeding grounds, which is a real problem in a humid Florida summer.

### Batteries and propane tanks

Car batteries can often be returned where you buy a new one. Many home improvement and electronics stores have drop-off bins for rechargeable batteries. For propane tanks, use a tank exchange program, ask a propane refill location, or bring them to your county's hazardous waste facility.

### Medical waste

Use a sharps container for needles and check with your pharmacy or county about how to dispose of it. Many pharmacies run medication take-back programs.
