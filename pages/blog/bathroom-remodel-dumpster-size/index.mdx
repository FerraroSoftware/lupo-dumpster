## What Comes Out of a Bathroom Remodel

A full bathroom gut usually includes:

- **Tile and mortar.** Floor tile, shower walls, and tub surrounds. Older Florida homes often have tile set in a thick mortar bed, which is much heavier than modern tile on backer board.
- **The tub or shower.** Fiberglass and acrylic units are light but bulky. Cast iron tubs can weigh a few hundred pounds on their own.
- **Vanity, countertop, and sink.** Particleboard vanities are light. Stone and cultured marble tops are heavy.
- **Toilet.** Porcelain is heavier than it looks.
- **Drywall and backer board.** Bathroom drywall that's been soaking up moisture for years can be noticeably heavier than new.
- **Fixtures and extras.** Mirrors, medicine cabinets, lights, exhaust fans, towel bars, and old flooring.

## Why Weight Matters More Than Space

On a bathroom job, you can hit the weight limit before the dumpster looks full. Tile, mortar, and cast iron are dense. A dumpster with a few feet of tile rubble in the bottom can weigh more than one heaped with old furniture.

### Signs You Should Size Up

- You're tearing out tile on the floor and the walls.
- There's a cast iron tub coming out.
- You're doing more than one bathroom.
- You're replacing flooring in the hallway or bedroom at the same time.
- You're also clearing out old cabinets, linens, or junk while the dumpster is there.

## Loading Tips for Bathroom Remodel Debris

How you load matters as much as the size you pick.

1. **Heavy items first, spread out.** Put tile, mortar, and the tub in first and spread them across the floor of the dumpster. Don't pile heavy debris in one corner.
2. **Break it down.** Take the vanity apart, cut drywall into flat sheets, and remove doors from cabinets. Flat pieces stack tight.
3. **Fill the gaps.** Use smaller debris, bags, and trim to fill spaces between larger pieces.
4. **Bag the dust and small stuff.** Grout, thinset crumbs, and broken tile shards are easier to handle in heavy-duty contractor bags.
5. **Empty the toilet.** Drain the tank and bowl before it goes in.
6. **Keep it level.** Don't load above the top edge. A level load is safer to haul.
7. **Set aside what can't go in.** Leftover liquid paint, solvents, and adhesives aren't accepted. Check with your county about household hazardous waste drop-off.

## Bathroom Remodels in Florida: A Few Local Notes

- **Look for hidden water damage.** Many bathrooms in Pasco and Pinellas homes have slow leaks behind tile. Expect some rotted subfloor or moldy drywall, and plan dumpster space for it.
- **Work around the weather.** In summer, afternoon storms roll through most days. Keep a tarp handy to cover the dumpster so drywall and debris don't soak up rain and gain weight.
- **Check permit rules.** Bathroom remodels that touch plumbing or electrical often need a building permit. A dumpster on your own driveway usually doesn't, but if it has to go on the street, check with your county or city first.
- **Talk to your HOA.** Some communities limit how long a dumpster can stay in a driveway.

## When to Have the Dumpster Delivered

Timing saves you money and hassle. Book delivery for the morning you start demo, not days ahead, so it isn't sitting empty in the driveway. Before it arrives:

- **Clear a spot.** Pick a flat, open area of the driveway close to the door you'll carry debris through. Move cars and check for low branches or wires.
- **Plan your path.** Lay down drop cloths or cardboard runners from the bathroom to the front door to protect floors.
- **Shut off the water.** Turn off the supply to the toilet and sink, and cap lines before pulling fixtures.
- **Line up help.** Tubs, vanity tops, and bags of tile are awkward. Two people make the carry safer.
