## 1. Sell Your Old Furniture

If the piece is in good shape, someone may pay for it. Solid wood dressers, dining sets, mid-century pieces, and outdoor furniture tend to sell well in the Tampa Bay area. Snowbird season is a good time to list, since seasonal residents are often furnishing condos and villas.

**Tips for a quick sale:**

- Clean it and take photos in daylight.
- Measure it and list the dimensions.
- Price it to move. If it hasn't sold in a week, drop the price.
- Meet in a public place for small items, and have a friend around when a buyer comes to the house.

**Skip selling if:** the piece is stained, broken, sagging, or smells like smoke or pets. You'll spend more time answering messages than it's worth.

## 2. Donate It to a Local Thrift Store or Charity

Many local thrift stores, charities, churches, and community groups in Pasco County accept furniture. Some offer pickup for larger pieces; others ask you to drop it off.

Before you load up the truck:

- **Call ahead.** Ask what they accept and whether they have room.
- **Expect them to be picky.** Most won't take pieces with stains, tears, broken frames, or pet damage.
- **Ask about upholstered items and mattresses.** Many places have limits on these.
- **Keep a receipt** if you plan to claim the donation on your taxes.

## 3. Curbside Bulk-Item Pickup

Some areas offer bulk-item pickup for large household items like couches and chairs. The rules vary a lot depending on whether you're in unincorporated Pasco County, inside a city like New Port Richey, or in a community with its own hauler.

Before you drag anything to the curb, check with your county or city about:

- Whether bulk pickup is offered at your address
- How to schedule it, and how far out the next pickup is
- How many items you can set out
- When you're allowed to put items out

Florida weather is the catch here. An upholstered couch that sits at the curb through an afternoon storm turns into a heavy, soggy mess, and your HOA may not love it sitting there either.
