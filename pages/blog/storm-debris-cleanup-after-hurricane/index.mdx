## Storm Debris Cleanup Starts With Safety

After a hurricane or tropical storm rolls through Pasco County and Tampa Bay, it's tempting to get outside and start dragging branches right away. Slow down first. Cleanup comes with its own hazards.

### Stay away from downed power lines

- **Treat every downed line as live.** Stay well back and keep kids and pets away.
- **Don't touch branches, fences, or puddles** that are in contact with a line.
- **Report downed lines** to your electric utility right away.
- **Watch for hidden lines** tangled in fallen trees and piles of debris.

### Use chainsaws carefully

Fallen oak limbs are heavy and often under tension. When you cut them, they can spring or roll.

- Wear eye and ear protection, gloves, long pants, and sturdy boots.
- Read the manual and only cut what you're comfortable with.
- Never cut above shoulder height or while standing on a ladder.
- Watch for limbs that are bent or pinned. They can snap back when cut.
- Leave trees on the house, near power lines, or leaning dangerously to a professional.

### Watch out for the heat and water

Post-storm Florida is hot, humid, and often without power. Drink plenty of water, take breaks in the shade, and work early in the morning. Watch for standing water, which can hide nails, broken glass, and holes. And keep an eye out for snakes, fire ants, and other critters displaced by flooding.

### Run generators outside only

If you're using a generator, keep it outdoors and well away from windows and doors. Carbon monoxide can build up quickly.

## Photograph Damage Before You Clean Up

Before you move a single branch, document everything. Your insurance company will want to see the damage as it was.

- **Take photos and video** of the outside of your home: roof, siding, windows, fences, pool cage, and lanai.
- **Capture the inside too:** water stains, wet carpet, damaged furniture.
- **Get wide shots and close-ups** so the scale is clear.
- **Photograph debris piles** before they're picked up, especially damaged building materials.
- **Keep receipts** for tarps, chainsaw rentals, dumpsters, and any other cleanup costs.

Once you've documented everything, contact your insurance company and ask what they need before you throw out damaged items. Some insurers may want to see certain items in person.

## Sorting Storm Debris for Curbside Pickup

After a major storm, counties in the Tampa Bay area often run curbside storm debris collection. The details change from storm to storm, so **follow your county's instructions** for your area. Check your county's website, local news, or social media pages for the current rules.

Counties usually ask residents to separate debris into piles by type. Here's how that typically breaks down:

| Debris type | Examples | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vegetative debris | Branches, oak limbs, palm fronds, leaves, logs | Often the largest pile after a storm |
| Construction and demolition debris | Drywall, carpet, roofing, fencing, lumber, furniture | Common after flooding or roof damage |
| Appliances | Refrigerators, washers, AC units | Empty fridges and freezers of food first |
| Household hazardous waste | Paint, chemicals, pool supplies, batteries | Usually collected separately |
| Electronics | TVs, computers | Often their own pile |
| Normal household trash | Bagged garbage | Goes out with regular trash, not storm debris |

Tips for curbside piles:

- **Don't block the road**, sidewalks, or storm drains.
- **Keep piles away** from mailboxes, fire hydrants, power lines, and utility boxes.
- **Don't bag vegetative debris** unless your county says to.
- **Be patient.** After a big storm, curbside pickup can take a while.
