## 1. Curbside Yard Waste Pickup

For most homes in unincorporated Pasco County and its cities, curbside yard waste collection is the first stop. It's convenient and often already part of what you pay for trash service.

The catch is the rules. Counties and haulers typically set limits on things like:

- How branches must be cut and bundled
- How long and heavy each bundle or piece can be
- Whether you need paper bags, cans, or loose piles
- How much you can put out per pickup
- Which day yard waste goes out

We're not going to quote those limits here, because they change and can depend on where you live. **Check Pasco County's solid waste information or call your hauler** before you set out a big pile. Debris that doesn't follow the rules may get left at the curb.

Curbside is great for weekly trimming. It's not built for clearing a half-acre of overgrown brush or a tree's worth of limbs.

## 2. Composting and Mulching

If you've got the space, some yard waste is worth keeping.

- **Leaves and grass clippings** break down quickly in Florida's heat and humidity. A simple bin or pile in a back corner of the yard works.
- **Small branches and twigs** can be chipped into mulch if you have access to a chipper.
- **Grass clippings** can often just stay on the lawn when you mow.

A few things to skip in the compost pile: palm fronds (they're fibrous and slow to break down) and anything diseased. Keep compost away from the house and fences, and check your HOA rules before you start one.

## 3. Hauling Yard Waste Yourself

If you have a pickup truck or a utility trailer, you can haul yard debris to a disposal site yourself. Pasco County operates solid waste facilities, and nearby counties have their own.

Before you load up:

- **Check the site's hours, accepted materials, and fees.** Look these up on the county website, since they change.
- **Tarp and strap your load.** Loose branches and fronds blowing off a truck on US-19 are a hazard to everyone behind you.
- **Count the trips.** A big job can turn into several runs, plus time, gas, and wear on your vehicle.

For a small one-time job, a self-haul can make sense. For a big one, it adds up fast.

## Timing Your Yard Cleanup in Pasco County

- **Spring** is a popular time, after oaks drop their leaves and before the summer heat sets in.
- **Before hurricane season** (June 1 through November 30), clearing dead branches and loose debris helps keep things from becoming projectiles. See our [hurricane prep checklist](/blog/hurricane-season-yard-prep-checklist).
- **After a storm,** the county may run separate storm debris collections with their own rules. Follow the county's instructions and don't mix storm debris with regular trash.
- **Work early in the day.** Summer afternoons are hot and often stormy.
