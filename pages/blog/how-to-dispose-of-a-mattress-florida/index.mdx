## Why Mattresses Are a Pain to Get Rid Of

A mattress doesn't fit in a trash can. It's floppy, heavy, and awkward to carry, especially down stairs. A queen or king can take two people just to get through a bedroom door.

In Florida, there's an extra wrinkle. Heat and humidity mean a mattress left in a garage or on a lanai can pick up moisture, mildew, and odors fast. And during the rainy season, a mattress left outside soaks up water like a sponge and gets even heavier.

That's why it pays to have a plan before you take the old one off the bed frame.

### 1. Donate it (only if it's in good condition)

If your mattress is fairly new, clean, and has no stains, rips, sagging, or pests, a local charity or shelter might take it. Be realistic, though. Many thrift stores and charities don't accept used mattresses at all because of hygiene rules.

Before you load it into the truck, call ahead and ask:

- Do you accept used mattresses and box springs?
- Do you require a tag or a certain condition?
- Do you offer pickup, or do I need to drop it off?

If they say no, don't leave it outside their door. That just creates a dumping problem for them.

### 2. Recycle it

Mattresses contain steel springs, foam, wood, and fabric, and a lot of that can be reused. Some areas have mattress recycling programs or facilities that take them.

Availability changes, so check locally. Your county's solid waste department is a good place to start. When our crew handles a [mattress disposal](/services/mattress-disposal) job, we send steel springs and metal frames to recycling whenever we can.

### 3. Use curbside bulk pickup

Many Florida counties and cities offer some kind of bulk item collection. But the details vary a lot:

- Some areas pick up bulk items on a set schedule.
- Some require you to call or book online first.
- Some limit how many items you can set out at a time.
- Some HOAs don't want items on the curb at all.

Pasco, Pinellas, Hillsborough, and Hernando counties each run things their own way, and cities inside them may have separate rules. Check with your county or city before you set a mattress out. A mattress left at the curb on the wrong day can sit in the rain for a week.

### 4. Bag it first if there are bedbugs

If you're getting rid of a mattress because of bedbugs, take a few extra steps so you don't spread them through the house or to someone else:

- **Seal it completely** in a heavy plastic mattress bag before you move it.
- **Tape the bag shut** and check for rips.
- **Label it** so no one picks it up off the curb and takes it home.
- **Don't donate or sell it.**
- **Tell your removal company** about the bedbugs ahead of time.

You can find mattress bags at most moving supply and home improvement stores.

## Tips for Moving a Mattress Safely

Whatever option you choose, the mattress has to get out of the house first. A few tips:

- **Get a helper.** Queens and kings are awkward even when they aren't heavy.
- **Wrap it in plastic.** It keeps it clean, makes it easier to grip, and protects it from rain.
- **Strip the bed and remove the frame first.** Take the headboard off so you have room to maneuver.
- **Go early.** Florida afternoons are hot and stormy. Moving a mattress at 8 AM is a lot nicer than at 3 PM.
- **Don't leave it outside for days.** A wet mattress gets heavy and can grow mildew.

## What Not to Do

- **Don't dump it.** Leaving a mattress on a vacant lot or back road is illegal dumping, and it's a mess for the neighborhood.
- **Don't burn it.** Foam and fabrics give off toxic smoke.
- **Don't set it out without checking pickup rules.** It may just sit there.
