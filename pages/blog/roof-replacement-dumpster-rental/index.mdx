## Why Weight Matters More Than Volume on a Roofing Job

Most dumpster decisions come down to space: how many pickup-truck loads of junk do you have? Roofing is different.

Asphalt shingles are dense. They're made of fiberglass or felt, asphalt, and mineral granules, and they come off in compact, flat layers. A pile of shingles that looks small on the driveway can weigh more than a trailer full of old furniture.

### Things that add weight

A few details push a roofing load heavier than you'd expect:

- **Multiple layers.** Some older Florida homes have a second layer of shingles nailed over the first. Two layers means roughly twice the debris.
- **Wet shingles.** A summer downpour soaks into old shingles and felt. Wet debris weighs more than dry debris.
- **Rotted decking.** If the roofer finds soft plywood, that wood goes in the dumpster too.
- **Tile or other heavy materials.** If you're not sure what's on your roof, tell us when you call.

### How to choose your roofing dumpster size

We won't give you a formula, because every roof is different. Instead, tell us:

1. Roughly how big the house is and whether it's one story or two.
2. How many layers of shingles are coming off.
3. Whether any decking is being replaced.
4. Whether anything else, like old gutters or a patio cover, is going in too.

With that, we can point you to the right size. If you're stuck between two, size up. One right-sized dumpster is almost always cheaper than a second haul. If your roofer is ordering the dumpster, ask them what they usually use on a house your size.

## Placement: Get the Dumpster Close to the House

On a tear-off, the crew wants to drop shingles straight from the roof edge into the dumpster. Every extra step between the roof and the trailer is time and mess.

Good placement looks like this:

- **On the driveway, as close to the house as possible.** Ideally right under the eave the crew is working from.
- **Clear overhead.** Watch for power lines, low oak branches, and palm fronds.
- **Room for us to back in and pull out.** Move cars, boats, and trailers out of the way the day of delivery.
- **Not on the grass if you can avoid it.** Florida's sandy soil can soften after rain, and a loaded trailer is heavy.

We set every trailer on protective boards to help keep your driveway safe. If you need to place it on the street, check with your county or city about whether a permit is required. ## Tarps, Nails, and Protecting Your Property

Roofing debris is messy. A little prep keeps it off your lawn, landscaping, and pool.

### Use tarps generously

Lay tarps along the foundation, over shrubs, and across any flower beds below the work area. Many roofers bring their own, but it doesn't hurt to ask. If you have a pool or lanai screen nearby, cover what you can and keep the pool cover on.

At the end of each day, a tarp over the dumpster keeps rain out of the load. That matters for the weight reasons above.

### Sweep for nails with a magnet

Thousands of roofing nails come off with the shingles, and some always end up in the yard and driveway. A rolling magnetic sweeper is the easiest fix. Ask your roofer to run one at the end of each day and again after cleanup. It's worth doing a final pass yourself, especially along the driveway, in the grass where kids or pets play, and near where the dumpster sat.

Nails in tires are no fun, and neither is stepping on one barefoot.

## Timing Your Roof Replacement Around Florida Weather

In Tampa Bay, the summer rainy season brings afternoon thunderstorms most days, and hurricane season runs from June 1 through November 30. Neither is great for having your roof open.

A few tips:

- **Aim for drier months if you can.** Many homeowners schedule roofs from late fall through spring, when storms are less frequent. That's also busy season for snowbirds, so book your roofer early.
- **Work in sections.** If the job has to happen in summer, a good crew tears off and dries in one section at a time so the house is never wide open when a storm rolls in.
- **Start early in the day.** Mornings are usually drier and cooler than afternoons.
- **Plan delivery for the day before.** Having the dumpster on site before the crew arrives means no waiting once tear-off starts.
- **Schedule pickup promptly.** Once the last shingles are in, call or text and we'll haul it away. A loaded dumpster sitting through a week of rain only gets heavier.

If a named storm is in the forecast, talk with your roofer about pausing the job and securing everything, including the dumpster's contents.
