Spring in Pasco County is short. One week it's perfect lanai weather, and the next you're running the AC all day. If you want to declutter without sweating through it, now's the time. Here's a simple plan to get your home cleared out and ready for summer.

## Why Spring Cleaning Matters More in Florida

Up north, spring cleaning is about shaking off winter. Here, it's about getting ready for what's coming:

- **Summer heat.** Garages and sheds get brutally hot by June. Sorting clutter in July is miserable.
- **Humidity and mildew.** Cardboard, fabric, and paper stored in humid spaces start to mildew and attract pests.
- **Hurricane season.** It runs June 1 through November 30. Loose items in the yard and lanai can become projectiles in high wind, and a cluttered garage leaves no room for shutters, a generator, or your car.
- **Snowbird timing.** If you split the year, spring is your last chance to clear things out before you head north.

## Your Spring Cleaning Junk Removal Checklist

Work one area at a time. Bring four bins or tarps: Keep, Donate, Sell, and Toss.

- [ ] **Lanai and patio:** faded cushions, cracked chairs, rusted grills, broken planters, worn outdoor rugs
- [ ] **Garage:** broken tools, empty boxes, old paint (set aside for proper disposal), outgrown bikes and toys
- [ ] **Closets:** clothes you haven't worn in a year, extra hangers, shoes, old linens
- [ ] **Kitchen and pantry:** expired food, duplicate gadgets, mismatched containers
- [ ] **Spare room and office:** old electronics (take these to an e-waste recycler), paperwork you've already scanned, broken furniture
- [ ] **Yard:** palm fronds, oak branches, dead shrubs, old pavers, leftover landscaping debris
- [ ] **Shed:** broken lawn equipment, old hoses, half-used chemicals (set aside for proper disposal)

## Start With the Lanai

The lanai is where Florida living happens, and it collects clutter fast. Sun and humidity are hard on outdoor furniture.

**Toss:**

- Cushions with mildew spots or a musty smell
- Plastic chairs that are faded, chalky, or cracked
- Rusted or wobbly metal furniture
- Grills that don't light, or that are rusted through
- Dead plants and cracked pots

Clean the screens and check for tears while the space is empty. A clear lanai also makes hurricane prep much faster, since everything outdoor has to come inside when a storm is coming.

## Clear Out the Garage

The garage is usually the biggest spring cleaning junk removal job. Pull everything out onto the driveway, sort it, and put back only what you use.

Watch for roaches, rodent droppings, and termite mud tubes along the walls as you go. Swap cardboard for lidded plastic bins and get them up off the slab.

Leave room near the door for hurricane supplies: shutters or plywood, a generator, gas cans, and water. For a full walk-through, see our [garage cleanout checklist](/blog/new-year-garage-cleanout-checklist).

## Tackle Closets and Bedrooms

Closets are quicker but easy to put off. Pull everything out, and:

- Donate clothes in good shape to local thrift stores or charities.
- Toss anything stained, stretched, or mildewed.
- Check the backs of closets for moisture or mildew, especially on exterior walls.
- Clear out old bedding, pillows, and that extra mattress in the guest room. Our [mattress disposal](/services/mattress-disposal) service handles those.

Old furniture you're replacing? Our guide on [how to get rid of old furniture](/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-old-furniture) covers selling, donating, and hauling it away.

## Don't Forget the Yard

Winter cold fronts knock down plenty of palm fronds and oak branches. Spring is a good time to:

- Trim dead fronds and branches before storm season
- Pull out dead shrubs and overgrown beds
- Clear out old landscaping materials

Yard waste, branches, and palm fronds are all accepted in a Lupo dumpster, so you can toss them in with household junk. If it's mostly yard debris, see our [yard waste removal](/services/yard-waste-removal) service.

## A Simple Weekend Spring Cleaning Plan

If you want to knock it all out at once, here's a schedule that works for most homes:

1. **Friday evening:** Walk through each area and stick a note on anything big that's leaving. Set out bins or tarps for Keep, Donate, Sell, and Toss.
2. **Saturday morning:** Start with the lanai and garage while it's cool. Load the heavy, bulky stuff first.
3. **Saturday afternoon:** Move indoors to closets, the spare room, and the kitchen while the heat peaks.
4. **Sunday morning:** Trim fronds and branches, clear beds, and toss yard debris.
5. **Sunday afternoon:** Drop off donations, list items to sell, and set aside anything that needs special disposal.

Drink plenty of water and take breaks. Even in March, a Florida afternoon in the sun can wear you down fast.

## What Can't Go in a Dumpster

A few common spring cleaning finds need special handling:

- Hazardous chemicals and solvents (pool chemicals, pesticides, cleaners)
- Wet paint
- Tires
- Batteries and propane tanks
- Medical waste

Check with your county or city about household hazardous waste drop-off days and locations.
