**Quick answer:** A good garage cleanout checklist works zone by zone: pull everything out, sort it into keep, donate, sell, and toss piles, then clean and put back only what earns its spot. January is a great time to tackle the garage in Pasco County. The weather is cool and dry, the bugs are quieter, and you've probably got a pile of empty holiday boxes sitting by the door. A Saturday in the 70s beats a July afternoon in a garage that feels like an oven.

Here's a simple plan you can follow from start to finish.

## Before You Start Your Garage Cleanout

A little prep keeps the job from stalling halfway.

- **Pick your weekend.** Block out two days for an average two-car garage. One day is often enough for a single-car garage.
- **Decide how the junk leaves.** Have a [dumpster](/services/dumpster-rental) in the driveway before you start, or schedule a junk removal pickup for the end of the weekend. Nothing kills momentum like a toss pile with nowhere to go.
- **Grab supplies.** Heavy-duty contractor bags, a few clear storage bins with lids, painter's tape and a marker for labels, work gloves, a dust mask, and a shop vac.
- **Set up four zones on the driveway.** Keep, Donate, Sell, and Toss. Use tarps or tape lines so piles don't blend together.

## The Zone-by-Zone Garage Cleanout Checklist

Work one area at a time. Empty it completely, sort it, then move on. Here's the checklist we suggest for most Florida garages.

| Zone | What to pull out | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry and walkway | Shoes, bags, recycling, holiday boxes | Empty boxes you're saving "just in case" |
| Shelving and wall storage | Bins, paint cans, decor, sports gear | Rust, mildew, warped cardboard, chewed bags |
| Workbench and tools | Hand tools, power tools, hardware | Duplicates, broken tools, dried-up glue and caulk |
| Lawn and garden | Mower, trimmer, hoses, pots, fertilizer | Cracked hoses, empty pots, old chemicals |
| Floor and corners | Old furniture, appliances, scrap lumber | Roaches, rodent droppings, termite mud tubes |
| Overhead and attic access | Holiday decor, luggage, keepsakes | Heat-damaged plastics, melted candles, brittle photos |
| Second fridge or freezer | Food, ice packs, the appliance itself | Is it still worth the electric bill? |

### Shelving and Wall Storage

Cardboard boxes are the first thing to go in a Florida garage. Humidity softens them, they wick moisture off the slab, and roaches love them. Move anything you're keeping into lidded plastic bins and raise them off the floor.

### Workbench and Tools

Keep one of each tool you actually use. Toss broken tools, rusted-through hardware, and dried-up tubes of caulk and glue. Spare working tools sell quickly on local marketplaces.

### Lawn and Garden

Check every bag of fertilizer, potting soil, and pool or lawn chemical. Florida heat breaks down a lot of products faster than you'd think. Partly used chemicals are not dumpster items; set them aside for your county's household hazardous waste drop-off.

### Floor and Corners

This is where pests hide. Look for droppings, nests, and the thin mud tubes termites build along walls and the slab. If you find signs of termites, call a pest control company before you put anything back.

## Keep, Donate, Sell, or Toss: How to Decide

When you're holding something and can't decide, ask:

1. **Have I used it in the last year?** If not, it's a donate or sell.
2. **Would I buy it again today?** If not, let it go.
3. **Is it damaged by heat, water, or pests?** Toss it. Mildewed fabric and warped particleboard don't come back.
4. **Is it worth more than the time to sell it?** If not, donate it.

Snowbird households have a bonus question: does this need to live in Florida at all? If it's been in the garage since you moved down and you haven't opened it, that's your answer.

### What Goes in the Toss Pile

Most garage junk can go straight into a dumpster: broken furniture, old shelving, scrap lumber, worn-out lawn chairs, rotted carpet remnants, and bagged trash. See the full list of [accepted items](/services/10-yard-dump-trailer) on our size pages.

A few things can't go in: hazardous chemicals and solvents, wet paint, tires, batteries, and propane tanks. Rules for these vary, so check with your county or city about drop-off options.

## After the Cleanout: Keep It Clean in the Florida Climate

Once the floor is clear, finish strong:

- **Sweep and vacuum** the slab and corners, then hose out the floor if it drains well.
- **Seal gaps** around the garage door and walls to slow down roaches and rodents.
- **Store up high.** Wall shelves and ceiling racks keep bins off a damp slab.
- **Use lidded bins, not cardboard.** Label everything.
- **Prep for hurricane season.** Set aside a clear spot near the door for shutters, plywood, a generator, and water before June 1.
